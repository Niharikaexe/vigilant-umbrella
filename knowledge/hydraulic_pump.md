## s3.1 Shaft Seal Leakage
Oil weeping at the drive shaft indicates shaft seal wear. A steady drip rather
than a film means the seal has failed and the unit must be taken out of service
before the reservoir level reaches the low-level switch. Typical causes are
case-drain back-pressure above 2 bar, contaminated fluid, or a seal past its
8000-hour service life. Replace with seal kit SEAL-A10-KIT. Running the pump
with a failed shaft seal risks an oil release to the deck or to water.

## s3.4 Pressure Loss Under Load
A gradual loss of system pressure under load with rising case-drain flow points
to worn pistons or a damaged swash plate. Measure case-drain flow: above 8% of
nominal displacement indicates internal wear beyond limits. Continued operation
accelerates wear and can seize the rotary group. Overhaul is required; this is
not a field repair.

## s3.7 Abnormal Noise and Cavitation
A rattling or knocking noise rising with speed usually indicates cavitation from
a restricted suction line, a clogged suction strainer, or fluid viscosity above
limits at cold start. Cavitation erodes the rotary group within hours. Stop the
unit, check suction restriction and fluid condition before restarting.

## s5.2 Overheating
Fluid temperature above 80 C degrades the fluid and the seals. Check cooler
airflow, bypass valve operation and fluid level. Sustained operation above 90 C
requires an immediate shutdown.
